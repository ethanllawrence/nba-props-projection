What this is

A hobby model projecting NBA player points, rebounds, assists, and PRA (points + rebounds + assists combined), compared against sportsbook lines, ranking any positive-expected-value edges — the same approach as the K Board's MLB strikeout model, adapted for basketball. Projections come from a model trained on real box scores, compared against FanDuel/DraftKings lines pulled each morning.

The model

Every projection comes from a machine-learning model (gradient-boosted trees, LightGBM) trained on three full seasons of box scores, 2023-24 through 2025-26, and retrained every run with the newest games. For each player it looks at: his recent form over short and long stretches (points, rebounds, assists, minutes, and the shot volume behind them, per game and per minute); how often he's been starting; days of rest and back-to-backs; home or away; the game's point spread and total, which capture pace, the expected score, and blowout risk; what tonight's opponent has been allowing; and which teammates are ruled out tonight, since their minutes and shots have to go somewhere. Separate models project points, rebounds, assists, PRA and minutes, and each projection comes with a spread of likely outcomes, so the site can put a probability on any line.

It was tested the honest way: train only on games before a given month, project that month blind, repeat for every month from November 2024 to June 2026, and compare to the real sportsbook lines from those games. The model was more accurate than the old recent-average method on every stat and more accurate than the books' opening lines, but the books' closing lines (after the day's news and money) were more accurate still. Most of the model's edge over opening lines came from reacting to injuries, so lines are pulled early in the day.

Today board

Table-only by design: a full slate runs 20+ meaningfully-projected players a night, so a sortable table beats scrolling cards. One row per player, one column each for points, rebounds, assists, and PRA; tap any header to sort by that projection. Players projected for fewer than 18 minutes are left off.

Injury checks

Lines are pulled early (best prices, boosts), so the board you bet from is the morning one. Injury news keeps coming after that: under NBA rules, teams file a game-day report around midday and must update a player's status as soon as a decision is made, so late scratches land in the hour or two before tip-off. The site saves the morning board and then re-checks ESPN rosters at 3 PM Arizona time and about an hour before each game tips. Each check re-runs the model and shows a box at the top of Today: who was ruled out (or cleared) since the morning, and whose projections moved because of it. Moved cells get a small ▲/▼ with the change. If nothing changed, the box says so. ESPN can lag the official report by a few minutes, so treat a quiet check right before tip-off as "probably fine," not a guarantee.

Edge coloring

Under each projection is the book line (FanDuel or DraftKings) and the model's chance of the over or under. That chance isn't the raw model alone: in testing, the raw model was overconfident (when it said 75%, the over hit about 57% of the time), so its view is blended with the chance implied by the sportsbook's prices, using weights fitted on the backtest. A cell is green when that blended chance of the over beats the price's break-even by 2 or more percentage points, red for the under, and gold when there's a line but no edge. Expect only a handful of colored cells a night; that's on purpose. (The old rule, coloring anything 6% off the line, would have lost about 6% of every dollar bet in the backtest.)

Parlay of the Day

Picked automatically every morning: 2-4 overs combined to roughly +200 to +300, overs only, so every leg is something to cheer for. Legs are the overs most likely to hit, not the best value: alt-line overs the model gives at least a 70% chance (the highest such alt line for the player) when the day's leftover odds budget bought alt lines, and main-line overs at 60%+. Each leg's chance is the model's blended probability (see Edge coloring), the model's own projection has to be above the line, and no leg is priced shorter than -350 (those barely move the payout). Because legs are ranked by likelihood rather than price, expect to lose roughly the sportsbook's cut over time; it's built for fun, not profit. Legs only come from players with steady minutes and no injury tag, never from a game with a double-digit spread, at most one per player and two per game. If it can't reach +150 without forcing weak legs, there's no parlay that day. In a replay of November 2025 with main lines only, that happened on 6 of 10 nights, and the 4 parlays it did make were mostly low assist and rebound overs; alt lines on better players are what make it work, and the budget buys a few games' worth each morning. The next morning each leg is graded against the real box score (a player who didn't play is void, like a sportsbook would do it), and the History section keeps the running record. Once picked, the legs never change during the day, since you may already have bet them. If a later injury check finds that a leg's player was ruled out or its projection moved against the leg, the leg gets a flag instead.

Jokic TD tracker

A separate tab for Nikola Jokic's triple-double prop. It leads with one call: bet the triple-double Yes tonight, or don't. His chance is halfway between his triple-double rate over his last 80 games and 50%. The call compares that chance to the sportsbook's Yes price: Yes when the price pays more than his chance deserves (for example +130, which needs 43%, when he's at 46%), No otherwise. With no price that day, it's Yes only above 50%.

Why not something fancier? We tested it. Since 10 points is nearly automatic for him (97% of games), a triple-double comes down to 10 rebounds and 10 assists on the same night, and the main model already projects both, so we drew them together from the model (they rise and fall together, mostly through minutes). Replayed on 2024-25 and 2025-26 with the model trained only on earlier games, it ran about 10 points low, and neither it nor a recent-form blend (the site's old method, which called only 41% of 2025-26 games right by chasing streaks) predicted his nightly triple-doubles better than a coin flip. On any given night he's close to 50/50. His raw 80-game rate ran about 5 points low in both seasons (his rate kept rising), and pulling it halfway to 50% scored as well as a coin flip with an average close to what actually happened, so that's the number shown, and the price is where any edge would be. The test script is in the repo (scripts/td_backtest.py) to re-run as seasons go on.

Results

Every morning, last night's board is graded against the real box scores. Two things are tracked. First, accuracy: how many points, rebounds, assists and PRA each projection missed by on average. Second, the edge calls: every green or red cell (see Edge coloring) counts as a call on the over or under. At standard -110 pricing a call needs to win 52.4% of the time just to break even, so that's the bar the hit rate is judged against. The board flips to the next day's slate at 8 PM Arizona time, but a night is only graded once all its games are over.

What's real vs. still a stub

Real: the projections and probabilities above, rosters and injury statuses from ESPN, and FanDuel/DraftKings lines pulled early each morning on a tight free-plan budget (the combined PRA line first, then points, rebounds and assists as credits allow, so on busy nights some stats show "no line"). Box scores are added to the model's data every morning, and injury checks run before tip-off. Still ahead: better minutes information (confirmed starting lineups), and a proper joint model for the Jokic triple-double call (still a simple hit-rate blend).
