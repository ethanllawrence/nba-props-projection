What this is

A hobby model projecting NBA player points, rebounds, assists, and PRA (points + rebounds + assists combined), built the same way as the K Board's MLB strikeout model and adapted for basketball. Projections come from a model trained on three seasons of real box scores and are compared against FanDuel/DraftKings lines pulled each morning. The site is honest about what the model can and can't do: it is a careful projection, not a proven way to beat the books (see The model and Results).

The model

Every projection comes from a machine-learning model (gradient-boosted trees, LightGBM) trained on three full seasons of box scores, 2023-24 through 2025-26, and retrained every run with the newest games. For each player it looks at: his recent form over short and long stretches (points, rebounds, assists, minutes, and the shot volume behind them, per game and per minute); how often he's been starting; days of rest and back-to-backs; home or away; the game's point spread and total, which capture pace, the expected score, and blowout risk; what tonight's opponent has been allowing; and which teammates are ruled out tonight, since their minutes and shots have to go somewhere. Separate models project points, rebounds, assists, PRA and minutes, and each projection comes with a spread of likely outcomes, so the site can put a probability on any line.

It was tested the honest way: train only on games before a given month, project that month blind, repeat for every month from November 2024 to June 2026, and compare to the real sportsbook lines from those games. The model was more accurate than the old recent-average method on every stat and more accurate than the books' opening lines, but the books' closing lines (after the day's news and money) were more accurate still. Most of the model's edge over opening lines came from reacting to injuries, so lines are pulled early in the day.

Today board

Table-only by design: a full slate runs 20+ meaningfully-projected players a night, so a sortable table beats scrolling cards. One row per player, one column each for points, rebounds, assists, and PRA; tap any header to sort by that projection. Players projected for fewer than 18 minutes are left off.

Next to each line: "hit 7/10" means he went over tonight's line in 7 of his last 10 games (green 7 or more, red 3 or fewer). It's a quick gut check, not part of the model: it ignores minutes, teammates out and the opponent, which the projection accounts for. The model's over or under chance (blended with the book's) shows when you hover or tap the projection.

Early look

The Today page also has an Early look tab: the next game day's projections, built by the same model from that day's rosters and the current injury reports, before any sportsbook lines exist. It refreshes with every daily run and looks up to 10 days ahead, so in the week before opening night it shows opening night. Treat it as a first read: injury news and lineups can still change, and the Today board (with lines and edge coloring) takes over at 8 PM Arizona time the night before.

Injury checks

Lines are pulled early (best prices, boosts), so the board you bet from is the morning one. Injury news keeps coming after that: under NBA rules, teams file a game-day report around midday and must update a player's status as soon as a decision is made, so late scratches land in the hour or two before tip-off. The site saves the morning board and then re-checks ESPN rosters at 3 PM Arizona time and about an hour before each game tips. Each check re-runs the model and shows a box at the top of Today: who was ruled out (or cleared) since the morning, and whose projections moved because of it. Moved cells get a small ▲/▼ with the change. If nothing changed, the box says so. ESPN can lag the official report by a few minutes, so treat a quiet check right before tip-off as "probably fine," not a guarantee.

Edge coloring

Each projection's color shows where it sits against the book line: red when it's below the line, gold when it's about even (within 3%), green when it's above, getting stronger up to 25% off the line. It's a quick read on direction, for example how comfortable a lower alt line looks. It is not a bet signal on its own: in the backtest, betting everything 6% or more off the line lost about 6% of every dollar, because the book's line already knows things the model doesn't.

The bet signal is the box. Under each projection is the line; hover or tap the projection for the model's chance of the over or under. That chance isn't the raw model alone: in testing, the raw model was overconfident (when it said 75%, the over hit about 57% of the time), so its view is blended with the chance implied by the sportsbook's prices, using weights fitted on the backtest. A number gets a box (green for over, red for under) when that blended chance beats the price's break-even by 2 or more percentage points. Expect only a handful of boxes a night; that's on purpose.

One exception: when the projection is 1.5 times the line or more, the cell turns grey and says "no call" instead of boxing an over, and the parlays skip that player and stat. A gap that big almost always means the book knows about a role or minutes change the model can't see yet (on Oct 21 the model had Isaiah Collier at 28.6 PRA, built on last March's games as a starter, against an 11.5 line). In the archive (58,329 main-line overs), overs at 1.5x or more hit no more often than the book expected and lost about 7% (PRA 14%), and those players played 20 minutes where the model had projected 23.

Parlay of the Day

One parlay per game day: 3 PRA overs at about +100 to +300 real DraftKings/FanDuel odds. It's picked the night before, at about 9:45 PM Arizona time, once the books have posted the next day's player lines (ESPN publishes DraftKings' lines and "N+" prices for free, usually by mid-evening), using the night's projections. If no lines were up yet, or more games have posted since, an 11:30 PM run picks again; games still without lines are listed as not in play.

How the legs are chosen (since Nov 2, 2026): the pool is the night's top projected players with 26+ projected minutes who aren't on the injury report, and since Nov 3 only stars: the book's PRA line must be 30.5 or more (a 2-3 week trial; on this season's saved boards that version went 5 of 11, and last season's replay showed star PRA legs on 30.5+ lines doing slightly better than smaller lines). A player qualifies when he's "green" on the Today board, meaning our PRA projection is above the book's line, and not 1.5x the line or more (see Edge coloring). Each leg is the book's line or an alt line below it (always below our projection), priced no shorter than -300, since a -400 leg barely moves the payout. Of every 3-leg combination, one leg per game, that lands at +100 to +300, the one with the most total green wins (green = how far the projection is over the book's line, as a share of the line). Ties go to the combination the book rates likeliest to hit. If no 3-leg combination fits, legs priced up to -350 are allowed; if there's still nothing, the parlay drops to 2 legs (same rules, up to -350), and the page says so. Only if even that fails is there no parlay that day. No game with a 12+ point spread.

Earlier versions: 3-4 overs at about +200 to +320, ranked by the model's chance minus the book's (Oct 20 - Nov 2; points or PRA only from Oct 30), went 2-11 through Nov 1. Replayed on this season's saved boards and prices (Oct 21 - Nov 1, using each day's last pre-tip board), the green rule would have gone 3-8, about even in units, with legs hitting 21 of 33 where the books' prices expected about 21. That is a small sample and no proof of an edge.

The track record shows the win-loss record and units: 1 unit on every graded parlay at its posted combined odds (a win at +250 is +2.5 units, a loss is −1). If a leg is voided because the player sat, the win is paid on the remaining legs' odds, as the books do.

The evening pick is a first look: the evening board can be missing players whose lines haven't posted yet. Since Nov 4, 2026, it stays provisional, and every run re-picks it from the current board, until the morning odds run (scheduled 6 AM; GitHub often starts it hours late, lately around 11 AM to 1 PM Arizona time). That run re-picks it from scratch with the full morning board and the same rules, locks it, and the page lists any legs that changed from the evening version. If nothing fits in the morning, the evening legs are kept unless a player is out or his line is gone. After the morning pick the legs don't change (you may have bet them); later runs only re-check it, flag injuries, and update the "last checked" time, which matches the Today board's update time. Any run that happens after the 8 PM board flip counts as an evening pick, so the morning always gets its re-pick. Each leg is graded the next morning against the box score (a player who didn't play is void, like a sportsbook would do it). Opening week used earlier versions: a "most likely overs" rule on Oct 20, and a morning pick Oct 21.

3PM model (beta)

The 3PM tab projects 3-pointers made in two parts, because they behave very differently. Attempts are steady: last season a player's three-point attempts per 36 minutes in half of his games predicted the other half almost perfectly (r = 0.97). Make rate is mostly noise: the same test on 3P% gave r = 0.2-0.3, and in 2023-26 it took about 400-650 attempts before a player's 3P% was even half skill (an older 2014 study said about 750). So:

Attempts come from a LightGBM model with the same inputs and training as the PRA model: minutes and role, recent attempts per minute (short and long memory), teammates out, pace and the game total, the spread, how many threes the opponent allows, and whether he's coming back from an absence (days since he last played, games missed lately, games since returning and how long he was out: first games back often come with a minutes limit). Defenses control how many threes opponents take far more than how many they make, so the opponent's 3P% allowed isn't used. Make rate is the player's recent 3P% (a 120-game half-life) pulled toward the average for his kind of shooter (by how often he shoots threes), as if he had 300 extra attempts at that average. The backtest picked 300: 150-500 were nearly as good, 800 and up clearly worse. Chances: attempts vary around the projection a bit more than a coin-flip count would, each attempt goes in at the make rate, and that gives the chance of 1+, 2+, 3+ threes. A small calibration per rung, fitted on earlier seasons, trims the 1+ and 2+ chances, which ran 2-4 points high.

Backtest (walk-forward: each month projected with a model trained only on earlier games), every player who played: 2025-26 average miss 0.88 threes made and 1.48 attempts; this season Oct 20 - Nov 6, 0.89 and 1.52. For comparison, KSplit's public 3PM diagnostics for the same window report 0.86 and 1.48 on a similar number of player-games. The coming-back-from-absence inputs cut the attempts error for players in their first three games back by 3-6% and their over-projection by more than half (2025-26: +0.17 attempts to +0.07). Players ESPN lists as out count as teammates out (more shots for everyone else). Day-to-day players are projected as if they play, because a prop on a player who sits is voided; their status is shown, and every run saves the statuses so a later test can decide how to treat them. The book's prices (DraftKings via ESPN, free) are saved every run, last price before tip-off, so the model can be tested against the market and blended with it after a few weeks. Until then the tab shows the gap between our chance and the book's but makes no picks. The table shows each player's projected threes, then DraftKings' over price for the safest alt under the line still priced -400 or longer, at the line, and one and two threes above it. Colors work like the Today board: gold where our chance and the book's agree, then green (we like it more) or red (less), full color at a 15-point gap; the projection takes the main line's color. Because 3PM lines are small, the gap is in chance points, not projection versus line. Its track record (average miss, and each rung's predicted chance vs how often it hit) is graded every morning. Like the Today board, an Early look shows the next game day's projections (model only, made after tonight's board) until the books post lines that morning; early-look projections aren't graded, only the game-day ones are.

Jokic TD tracker

A separate tab for Nikola Jokic's triple-double prop. It leads with one call: bet the triple-double Yes tonight, or don't. His chance is halfway between his triple-double rate over his last 80 games and 50%. The call compares that chance to the sportsbook's Yes price: Yes when the price pays more than his chance deserves (for example +130, which needs 43%, when he's at 46%), No otherwise. With no price that day, it's Yes only above 50%.

Why not something fancier? We tested it. Since 10 points is nearly automatic for him (97% of games), a triple-double comes down to 10 rebounds and 10 assists on the same night, and the main model already projects both, so we drew them together from the model (they rise and fall together, mostly through minutes). Replayed on 2024-25 and 2025-26 with the model trained only on earlier games, it ran about 10 points low, and neither it nor a recent-form blend (the site's old method, which called only 41% of 2025-26 games right by chasing streaks) predicted his nightly triple-doubles better than a coin flip. On any given night he's close to 50/50. His raw 80-game rate ran about 5 points low in both seasons (his rate kept rising), and pulling it halfway to 50% scored as well as a coin flip with an average close to what actually happened, so that's the number shown, and the price is where any edge would be. The test script is in the repo (scripts/td_backtest.py) to re-run as seasons go on.

Results

Every morning, last night's board is graded against the real box scores. Two things are tracked. First, accuracy: how many points, rebounds, assists and PRA each projection missed by on average. Second, the edge calls: every green or red cell (see Edge coloring) counts as a call on the over or under. At standard -110 pricing a call needs to win 52.4% of the time just to break even, so that's the bar the hit rate is judged against. The board flips to the next day's slate at 8 PM Arizona time, but a night is only graded once all its games are over.

Data and schedule

Rosters, injury statuses, schedules and box scores come from ESPN. Sportsbook lines come first from ESPN, which publishes DraftKings' player lines and "N+" prices for points, rebounds, assists and PRA for free, usually by the evening before; every run pulls them. A free odds plan (about 16 credits a day once baseball is over; each stat costs 1 credit per game) only buys what ESPN doesn't have on the 6 AM run: a game or stat it skipped, in this order: PRA lines, alt-line prices for the games with the top projected players, then points, rebounds and assists lines. Unused credits carry over. No sportsbook is preferred; the board shows whichever line the data came from. If a free PropLine key is set up, main lines come from there too at no cost. The site updates at 6 AM Arizona time (lines, last night's grading, the Parlay of the Day check), 3 PM, and about an hour before each game tips (injury checks); it flips to the next day's slate at 8 PM, and picks the next day's Parlay of the Day at 9:45 PM (and 11:30 PM if needed).

Known limits: early in a season the model leans on last season's games (including the playoffs), so projections for stars can run low for the first few weeks, and players who changed teams are projected in their old role until they've played a few games. Still ahead: confirmed starting lineups, which would sharpen minutes projections more than anything else.
