Today Jokic TD Parlay Methodology

What this is (planned)

A hobby model projecting NBA player points, rebounds, assists, and PRA (points + rebounds + assists combined), compared against sportsbook lines, ranking any positive-expected-value edges — the same approach as the K Board's MLB strikeout model, adapted for basketball. This page, like the rest of this site, is currently a design mockup: the layout and interactions are real, the data is not.

Today board

Table-only by design, not cards: a full NBA slate runs 20+ meaningfully-projected players a night (vs. the K Board's ~15 probable starters), so a scannable sortable table beats scrolling through cards. One row per player, one column each for points, rebounds, assists, and PRA — tap any of those headers to sort the whole slate by that stat's projection, so "who's projected for the most points/rebounds/assists/PRA tonight" is a single tap away. PRA is currently just the sum of the three individual lines rather than a separately-priced combo line; that'll change once real odds are wired in and a book actually posts one.

Edge coloring

Each projected number is colored by how far it sits from that stat's book line, as a percentage of the line: green when the projection clears the line by 6% or more (model likes the over), red when it's 6% or more under the line (model likes the under), and gold in between, where the model roughly agrees with the market. A relative (percentage) threshold, rather than a fixed point gap, is what lets one rule work sensibly across points, rebounds, assists and PRA despite their very different typical ranges. The small number under each projection is that stat's current book line, priced off FanDuel or DraftKings (occasionally bet365) rather than shopped across a wide book list, since those are the only ones actually in play day to day.

Jokic TD tracker

A separate tab tracking Nikola Jokic's triple-double prop specifically — his favorite bet and a fun one to track on its own, given how often it hits. The page leads with a single call: bet the triple-double yes or no tonight, based on whether the model gives it better or worse than even odds — that call matters more here than the edge math, so it's the headline rather than a footnote. The model probability and market price sit underneath as supporting detail. The season summary and recent-games log below that are Jokic's real 2025-26 numbers; the "Bet TD" call itself is a placeholder until a real triple-double model exists (a proper one needs a joint probability across points/rebounds/assists, not three independent point estimates multiplied together — see the planning doc for more on why that's harder than it sounds).

Parlay of the Day

A separate tab surfacing 3-4 alt-line legs, combined to roughly +200 to +300 odds. This isn't an expected-value play the way the Today board's edges are — it's specifically about picking legs where the model has real conviction: the projection clears not just the full book line but the shaded alt line by a solid margin, and the player's minutes look stable (low blowout risk, no role uncertainty). Each leg shows the reasoning behind it, and the History section tracks whether each day's parlay actually hit, since a parlay needs every leg to hit — a good record here proves out the "high conviction" claim or it doesn't.

What's real vs. still a stub

Nikola Jokic's row on the Today board and his "Bet TD" call are genuine model output — a recency-weighted rolling average over his real 2025-26 game log, computed and exported by the actual Python pipeline (nproj/). Everyone else's numbers, every book line and every Parlay of the Day leg are still hand-typed mockup data. The real blocker on expanding that: stats.nba.com isn't reachable from the development sandbox this project is currently built in, so full-league backfill needs to run somewhere with normal internet access (a GitHub Actions workflow, already scaffolded and waiting) before it can happen for real. Still ahead after that: live sportsbook lines via The Odds API, a real (LightGBM) model in place of the rolling-average baseline, automated Parlay-of-the-Day leg selection (right now the daily pick is manually curated), a real day-rollover clock, and a settlement/results/performance page.

See the planning doc for the full build-phase roadmap and open decisions.
