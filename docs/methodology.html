What this is

A hobby model projecting NBA player points, rebounds, assists, and PRA (points + rebounds + assists combined), built the same way as the K Board's MLB strikeout model and adapted for basketball. Projections come from a model trained on three seasons of real box scores and are compared against FanDuel/DraftKings lines pulled each morning. The site is honest about what the model can and can't do: it is a careful projection, not a proven way to beat the books (see The model and Results).

The model

Every projection comes from a machine-learning model (gradient-boosted trees, LightGBM) trained on three full seasons of box scores, 2023-24 through 2025-26, and retrained every run with the newest games. For each player it looks at: his recent form over short and long stretches (points, rebounds, assists, minutes, and the shot volume behind them, per game and per minute); how often he's been starting; days of rest and back-to-backs; home or away; the game's point spread and total, which capture pace, the expected score, and blowout risk; what tonight's opponent has been allowing; and which teammates are ruled out tonight, since their minutes and shots have to go somewhere. Separate models project points, rebounds, assists, PRA and minutes, and each projection comes with a spread of likely outcomes, so the site can put a probability on any line.

It was tested the honest way: train only on games before a given month, project that month blind, repeat for every month from November 2024 to June 2026, and compare to the real sportsbook lines from those games. The model was more accurate than the old recent-average method on every stat and more accurate than the books' opening lines, but the books' closing lines (after the day's news and money) were more accurate still. Most of the model's edge over opening lines came from reacting to injuries, so lines are pulled early in the day.

Today board

Table-only by design: a full slate runs 20+ meaningfully-projected players a night, so a sortable table beats scrolling cards. One row per player, one column each for points, rebounds, assists, and PRA; tap any header to sort by that projection. Players projected for fewer than 18 minutes are left off.

Early look

The Today page also has an Early look tab: the next game day's projections, built by the same model from that day's rosters and the current injury reports, before any sportsbook lines exist. It refreshes with every daily run and looks up to 10 days ahead, so in the week before opening night it shows opening night. Treat it as a first read: injury news and lineups can still change, and the Today board (with lines and edge coloring) takes over at 8 PM Arizona time the night before.

Injury checks

Lines are pulled early (best prices, boosts), so the board you bet from is the morning one. Injury news keeps coming after that: under NBA rules, teams file a game-day report around midday and must update a player's status as soon as a decision is made, so late scratches land in the hour or two before tip-off. The site saves the morning board and then re-checks ESPN rosters at 3 PM Arizona time and about an hour before each game tips. Each check re-runs the model and shows a box at the top of Today: who was ruled out (or cleared) since the morning, and whose projections moved because of it. Moved cells get a small ▲/▼ with the change. If nothing changed, the box says so. ESPN can lag the official report by a few minutes, so treat a quiet check right before tip-off as "probably fine," not a guarantee.

Edge coloring

Under each projection is the book line (FanDuel or DraftKings) and the model's chance of the over or under. That chance isn't the raw model alone: in testing, the raw model was overconfident (when it said 75%, the over hit about 57% of the time), so its view is blended with the chance implied by the sportsbook's prices, using weights fitted on the backtest. A cell is green when that blended chance of the over beats the price's break-even by 2 or more percentage points, red for the under, and gold when there's a line but no edge. Expect only a handful of colored cells a night; that's on purpose. (The old rule, coloring anything 6% off the line, would have lost about 6% of every dollar bet in the backtest.)

Parlay of the Day

Picked automatically every morning with the same rules as the early look parlay below, applied to that day's prices: 3-4 overs at about +200 to +320 real FanDuel/DraftKings odds, from the night's top projected players, no game with a 12+ point spread. Prices come from, oldest to newest, the early-look alt ladders bought the day before (PRA and points), the morning's main lines, and the morning's alt ladders; a newer price for the same line wins. (Opening day, Oct 20, used an older "most likely overs" rule that ranked legs by the model's chance alone; it picked role players' rebound and assist overs.)

Once picked, the legs don't change during the day, since you may already have bet them. One exception: a manual run with the odds box ticked, after the morning lines were bought, is a top-up. It buys fresh alt ladders (all four stats) for games that haven't started, re-picks the parlay from games tipping at least 15 minutes later, and buys the next game day's early-look ladders, all from a fresh day's budget. The page then shows the morning pick as replaced; only the re-pick is graded. If a later injury check finds that a leg's player was ruled out or its projection moved against the leg, the leg gets a flag. Each leg is graded the next morning against the real box score (a player who didn't play is void, like a sportsbook would do it).

Early look parlay

A second parlay for the next game day: 3-4 star overs at about +200 to +320 real sportsbook odds, built from books and model together. On the morning run, once that game day is within two days, the site buys FanDuel/DraftKings alt-line prices for PRA and points (a few credits from the day's spare budget, games with the top projected players first). The pool is the night's top 30 players by projected PRA, with 30+ projected minutes and not on the injury report. Players who just changed teams sit out until 3 of their last 5 games are with the new team, because the model still sees their old role. Every alt over priced between -400 and -120 that the model projects the player to clear gets two chances. The book's is its price with the book's cut removed. The model's is adjusted by a correction fitted on two seasons of star milestones, since the raw model runs a little cautious on stars. Any leg the model rates 10+ points below the book is dropped. Of the rest, the site takes the 3-4 leg combination (one leg per player, at most three per game) whose real combined odds land at +200 to +320 and that the model likes most relative to the book. If nothing lands there, it takes the best one from +150 to +200; below that, there's no early parlay. It's graded the next morning.

Why the model works as a filter: on archived main lines for star overs (7,057 bets), when the model rated the over 10+ points below the book, it hit 44.5% against the book's 49.8%, a 16% loss. 3-10 points below did about as well as the book (-5%, roughly the book's normal cut), and 3-8 points above hit 54% (+1.8%). So the model is most useful for avoiding traps. It isn't proven to beat the books. A wider re-check (14,396 star overs, Oct 20) showed bigger gaps aren't bigger edges: +3 to +8 hit 53.1% vs the book's 49.9%, +8 to +15 hit 53.0%, and +15 or more hit 52.0%, even though the model claimed 61% and 71% for those. A huge gap usually means the book knows something the model can't see, like a minutes limit. So when ranking combinations, any gap above +8 counts as +8, and ties go to the combination the book itself rates likeliest to hit. Two earlier versions taught the price rules. Picking legs by model chance alone led to legs the books priced at -1100 to -7000. Then a -300 to -150 price window missed the real star legs, because alt ladders move 5 points at a time and prices jump (Brunson 30+ PRA -390, 35+ -146).

Jokic TD tracker

A separate tab for Nikola Jokic's triple-double prop. It leads with one call: bet the triple-double Yes tonight, or don't. His chance is halfway between his triple-double rate over his last 80 games and 50%. The call compares that chance to the sportsbook's Yes price: Yes when the price pays more than his chance deserves (for example +130, which needs 43%, when he's at 46%), No otherwise. With no price that day, it's Yes only above 50%.

Why not something fancier? We tested it. Since 10 points is nearly automatic for him (97% of games), a triple-double comes down to 10 rebounds and 10 assists on the same night, and the main model already projects both, so we drew them together from the model (they rise and fall together, mostly through minutes). Replayed on 2024-25 and 2025-26 with the model trained only on earlier games, it ran about 10 points low, and neither it nor a recent-form blend (the site's old method, which called only 41% of 2025-26 games right by chasing streaks) predicted his nightly triple-doubles better than a coin flip. On any given night he's close to 50/50. His raw 80-game rate ran about 5 points low in both seasons (his rate kept rising), and pulling it halfway to 50% scored as well as a coin flip with an average close to what actually happened, so that's the number shown, and the price is where any edge would be. The test script is in the repo (scripts/td_backtest.py) to re-run as seasons go on.

Results

Every morning, last night's board is graded against the real box scores. Two things are tracked. First, accuracy: how many points, rebounds, assists and PRA each projection missed by on average. Second, the edge calls: every green or red cell (see Edge coloring) counts as a call on the over or under. At standard -110 pricing a call needs to win 52.4% of the time just to break even, so that's the bar the hit rate is judged against. The board flips to the next day's slate at 8 PM Arizona time, but a night is only graded once all its games are over.

Data and schedule

Rosters, injury statuses, schedules and box scores come from ESPN. Sportsbook lines come from FanDuel/DraftKings through a free odds plan with a tight monthly budget (about 16 credits a day once baseball is over; each stat costs 1 credit per game). The morning run projects first, then buys in this order: the PRA line for every game; fresh PRA and points alt-line prices for the games with the top projected players (what the Parlay of the Day is priced from); then points, rebounds and assists lines, top games first. About a third of the budget is held for the next game day's early-look prices. On busy nights that usually means PRA lines everywhere and few or no points, rebounds and assists lines; a note above the board says so. If a free PropLine key is set up, main lines come from there first at no cost. The site updates at 6 AM Arizona time (lines, last night's grading), 3 PM, and about an hour before each game tips (injury checks), and flips to the next day's slate at 8 PM.

Known limits: early in a season the model leans on last season's games (including the playoffs), so projections for stars can run low for the first few weeks, and players who changed teams are projected in their old role until they've played a few games. Still ahead: confirmed starting lineups, which would sharpen minutes projections more than anything else.
