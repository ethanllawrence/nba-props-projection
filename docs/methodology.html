What this is (planned)

A hobby model projecting NBA player points, rebounds, assists, and PRA (points + rebounds + assists combined), compared against sportsbook lines, ranking any positive-expected-value edges — the same approach as the K Board's MLB strikeout model, adapted for basketball. Projections come from real game logs; sportsbook lines are not wired in yet (see the last section).

Today board

Table-only by design, not cards: a full NBA slate runs 20+ meaningfully-projected players a night (vs. the K Board's ~15 probable starters), so a scannable sortable table beats scrolling through cards. One row per player, one column each for points, rebounds, assists, and PRA — tap any of those headers to sort the whole slate by that stat's projection, so "who's projected for the most points/rebounds/assists/PRA tonight" is a single tap away. PRA is currently just the sum of the three individual lines rather than a separately-priced combo line; that'll change once real odds are wired in and a book actually posts one.

Edge coloring

Each projected number is colored by how far it sits from that stat's book line, as a percentage of the line: green when the projection clears the line by 6% or more (model likes the over), red when it's 6% or more under the line (model likes the under), and gold in between, where the model roughly agrees with the market. A relative (percentage) threshold, rather than a fixed point gap, is what lets one rule work sensibly across points, rebounds, assists and PRA despite their very different typical ranges. The small number under each projection is that stat's current book line, priced off FanDuel or DraftKings (occasionally bet365) rather than shopped across a wide book list, since those are the only ones actually in play day to day.

Parlay of the Day

Picked automatically every morning, 2-4 legs combined to roughly +200 to +300. This isn't an expected-value play like the Today board's edges; it's about conviction. For each player with a posted line, the model turns his recent games into a chance of clearing that line (then pulls that chance 15% back toward a coin flip, since a 20-game sample overstates certainty). A leg qualifies at 62% or better, only for players with steady minutes and no injury tag, and never from a game with a double-digit point spread, where starters often sit the fourth quarter. When the day's odds budget has credits left, it also buys alt-line prices for the best few candidates and uses the highest alt line the model is about 80% sure of, at a shorter price. Legs are added in order of model confidence, at most one per player and two per game, until the price reaches the target; if it can't get there without forcing weak legs, there's no parlay that day. The next morning each leg is graded against the real box score (a player who didn't play is void, like a sportsbook would do it), and the History section keeps the running record.

Jokic TD tracker

A separate tab tracking Nikola Jokic's triple-double prop specifically — his favorite bet and a fun one to track on its own, given how often it hits. The page leads with a single call: bet the triple-double yes or no tonight, based on whether the model gives it better or worse than even odds — that call matters more here than the edge math, so it's the headline rather than a footnote. The model probability and market price sit underneath as supporting detail. The season summary and recent-games log below that are Jokic's real 2025-26 numbers; the triple-double probability itself is a real but deliberately simple placeholder (a blend of his season hit rate and recent-game hit rate) until a proper joint probability model across points/rebounds/assists exists, rather than three independent point estimates multiplied together — see the planning doc for more on why that's harder than it sounds.

What's real vs. still a stub

Every projection on the Today board is real model output: a recency-weighted average of each player's game log (this season plus last, so early-season numbers aren't built on three games), pulled from ESPN's public stats feed by a GitHub Actions job that runs twice a day. Players listed as out, or averaging under 20 minutes, are left off the board. The Jokic tab's season summary, recent games and triple-double call come from the same feed. Book lines come from FanDuel and DraftKings via The Odds API, pulled once each morning on a tight free-plan budget: the combined PRA line first, then points, rebounds and assists as credits allow, so on busy nights some stats show "no line" and stay uncolored. Still ahead: a real (LightGBM) model in place of the rolling-average baseline, a settled day-rollover clock, and a results/performance page for the Today board.
